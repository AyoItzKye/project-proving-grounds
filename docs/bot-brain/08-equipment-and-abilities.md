# Equipment and abilities deep dive

Every pickup equipment item (Halo 3), armor ability (Reach, Halo 4), loadout perk and powerup, with the standard use, the advanced use, and the sneaky uses most players never learn. Each entry ends with how to counter it and what the bot should do.

## Halo 3 equipment in depth

Halo 3 equipment is a single-use pickup carried in one slot (picking up a second item drops the first). It deploys in front of the player with a short throw, can be deployed mid-air, despawns after a while if unused, and drops on death. The last point is the first sneaky rule: a dead enemy's equipment is lying where they died, and so is a dead teammate's, so the bot checks corpses for equipment the way it checks them for grenades.

### Bubble Shield

- Basics: a dome about 15 ft across that blocks all projectiles, explosions and vehicles for about 20 s; players walk through it freely.
- Advanced: deploy it on the flag carrier mid-run; deploy it on a lift landing or man cannon landing so arrivals are safe; deploy it in a doorway as a 20 s wall that snipers and BRs cannot shoot through.
- Sneaky: vehicles cannot pass; a Warthog, Ghost or Chopper slams into it and stops, which lines its crew up for a stick or boarding. Grenades thrown from inside leave the dome, so a bot inside can nade out while nothing comes in. A sword lunge from outside connects through the dome (lunges are melee, not projectiles), and the shotgun does not, so the dome's answer is the sword, not the shotgun. The dome also blocks the Spartan laser and rockets, which makes it the best vehicle-crew escape on Valhalla and Sandtrap: bail out next to the dome. Dropped on the Oddball or on a bomb plant it buys 20 s of arming time.
- Counters: walk in and melee; throw a grenade inside; a Power Drain thrown inside strips everyone's shields; wait outside with a shotgun or sword; grav lift the players out. Never shoot the dome from outside.
- Bot rule: deploy only when the shield is popped and no cover is within 10 ft, or on an objective carrier or plant; step out to shoot; expect the enemy to come inside.

### Power Drain

- Basics: a thrown device that pulses for about 4 s, draining shields of anyone in a 15 ft radius and stalling vehicles.
- Advanced: throw it into a room before pushing (everyone inside is one head shot from death); throw it at a stopped Warthog for a free board; throw it onto a hill or flag stand to clear it.
- Sneaky: it drains the thrower too, so throw and back out of the radius. It bounces, so aim at the floor beside a corner to roll it around it. A Power Drain landing inside a Bubble Shield turns the dome into a trap for whoever is in it. Thrown under a Hornet or Banshee hovering low it stalls the aircraft into the ground. Thrown onto an Overshield carrier it strips the overshield too.
- Counters: step out of the radius immediately (it is slow to fully drain); shoot the device to destroy it; armor-wise nothing resists it.
- Bot rule: throw it as the opener of any room push and any boarding attempt; never throw it when a teammate is in the radius.

### Regenerator

- Basics: a field about 15 ft across that recharges shields rapidly for about 15 s for anyone inside, both teams.
- Advanced: drop it at your feet in a fight you are winning at Melee or Short band; drop it on a sniper perch so the sniper can trade and recharge; drop it on the flag stand while defending.
- Sneaky: it does not block fire, so it is bait: enemies run into it to heal and stand still in a known spot. It out-heals a single BR, so a bot inside wins a 1v1 against a bot outside. A Power Drain thrown into a Regenerator cancels the heal; a grenade does not. Two bots inside a Regenerator can melee-trade and both survive.
- Counters: do not fight someone standing inside it from outside; throw a Power Drain or grenades in, or step inside too (it heals you as well) and melee.
- Bot rule: use it when the team is holding an objective or when the bot is in a winning close fight; never drop it when losing (it heals the enemy who pushes in).

### Trip Mine

- Basics: placed on the ground, beeps for about 3 s, then detonates when anyone or any vehicle touches it; one-shot kill on players and light vehicles; it can be shot to detonate.
- Advanced: place it on the flag's return route, on a vehicle choke, on the Warthog's path on Valhalla, on the Elephant's ramp, on a lift pad (the rider dies on arrival), around a corner the enemy must round.
- Sneaky: a Trip Mine can be placed on a moving vehicle (the Elephant, a Warthog's hood): the vehicle becomes a bomb that kills whoever boards it. Placed under a dropped flag or Oddball it kills the pickup. Placed on top of a Bubble Shield's spawn or an Overshield spawn it kills the grabber. Thrown into a Gravity Lift it launches and lands wherever the lift throws it. It is invisible on radar; the beeping is the only warning. A Trip Mine next to a sticky-grenaded player detonates with them.
- Counters: shoot it from range; listen for the beep; never walk a known vehicle route without checking the ground; a Bubble Shield blocks its blast.
- Bot rule: place on routes the enemy must use and leave; never stand within 20 ft of a placed mine.

### Deployable Cover

- Basics: a shield wall about 10 ft wide that blocks shots in both directions until destroyed by sustained fire or explosives.
- Advanced: drop it across a sniper lane to cross safely; drop it in a doorway to funnel enemies to the other door; drop it in front of a mounted turret to protect the gunner.
- Sneaky: players can stand and jump on it, so it is a step to reach ledges (The Pit's walls, Guardian's rails, Narrows' bridge side) and to boost a teammate. It blocks the Spartan laser. Dropped on a flag stand it hides the flag from the sniper lane. Dropped on a man cannon landing it stops arrivals dead in front of a shotgun. Grenades land behind it if arced, and a Brute Shot bounces targets over it.
- Counters: shoot it down (about a magazine of BR), rocket it, or walk around; grenades over the top.
- Bot rule: use to cross a known sniper lane or to block the second entrance to a held room.

### Radar Jammer

- Basics: a thrown device that fills every motion tracker within range, both teams, with fake red dots for about 15 s.
- Advanced: throw before a push so the defenders cannot read the approach; throw next to the flag carrier so chasers lose the dot.
- Sneaky: it jams friendlies too, so throw it where the team is not relying on radar. It is loud and visible, so it also works as bait: enemies come to shoot it. In no-radar competitive settings it does nothing and MLG removed it.
- Counters: trust line of sight; shoot the device; move away from it.
- Bot rule: throw it when the plan is a flank or a flag pull and the enemy is likely watching radar; ignore it in no-radar variants.

### Flare

- Basics: a thrown light that whites out the screen of anyone looking at it within range for about 3 s.
- Advanced: throw at a sniper perch before peeking; throw into a camped room before entering; throw at a turret gunner.
- Sneaky: it blinds the thrower if they look at it, so throw and look away or turn the corner. Throw it behind you while retreating: the chaser runs into the light. A Flare inside a Bubble Shield blinds everyone inside. Thrown into the sky over a Banshee pilot it blinds them mid-strafe.
- Counters: turn away, back into cover, listen for footsteps; it does not damage.
- Bot rule: throw before a push into a held room or against a scoped sniper; turn away for the first second.

### Gravity Lift

- Basics: a deployed lift that throws players, vehicles and objects upward for about 15 s.
- Advanced: reach rooftops and ledges without the ramps (The Pit's sword room roof, Guardian's top mid from Blue room, Valhalla's base roof, Sandtrap's Elephant roof); launch a teammate onto a sniper perch.
- Sneaky: it launches enemies too: drop it under an enemy standing still (a sniper, a hill holder, a flag defender) and they are thrown into the air with no strafe. Grenades and Trip Mines thrown into it fly up and over walls: a frag into a grav lift lobs onto a roof. A Mongoose or Warthog driven into it jumps walls and the river on Valhalla. On Sandtrap it can lift the Elephant's wheels and unstick it. Place it in a doorway and nobody can walk through without being launched into the ceiling (a stun and an easy shot).
- Counters: shoot anyone riding it; grenade the landing; avoid the doorway it sits in; it can be destroyed with fire.
- Bot rule: use for a planned rooftop setup or to launch a stationary enemy; never ride one into a watched lane.

### Invincibility, Cloaking, Auto Turret (campaign and Forge only)

- Invincibility (Brute Chieftain): about 10 s of no damage; rush a power weapon or a plant. Counter: run; the glow ends.
- Cloaking and Auto Turret appear in campaign and some custom games; the cloak is a Reach-style camo, the turret a stationary gun. Bots in custom games treat them as camo and a sentry.

### Equipment economy rules

- One slot: picking up a second item drops the first where the bot stands, so the bot does not grab equipment it does not want when carrying something better.
- Timers: map equipment respawns on 60 to 120 s timers in most variants (Regenerator at The Pit's sniper tower, Bubble Shield on Valhalla's base, Power Drain on Guardian's bottom); the bot counts them like weapons.
- Deploy time: about 0.5 s with a throw animation; the bot cannot shoot during it, so it deploys behind cover.
- Mid-air: equipment can be deployed while jumping or falling, so a Bubble Shield can be popped at the top of a man cannon arc and a grav lift dropped while falling into a pit.

## Halo: Reach armor abilities in depth

Abilities are chosen at spawn (loadouts), recharge over time, and are the defining feature of Reach fights. Each has a well-known use and two or three interactions the bot can exploit that most players never learn.

### Sprint

- Basics: about 1.6x run speed for several seconds; cannot shoot; the stop takes a moment.
- Advanced: sprint-jump covers gaps the normal jump cannot; sprint to rotate between objectives; sprint out of a losing fight once line of sight is broken (sprinting in the open is death).
- Sneaky: the melee out of a sprint comes a hair faster than from a walk if the bot releases sprint at 10 ft; sprinting is loud (breathing), so a sprinter announces itself in no-radar games; a sprinting flag carrier moves at full sprint speed with the flag, which makes sprint the best flag-running ability on open maps. Sprint cancels the end of a reload animation only after the magazine is in, so the bot can reload, sprint at the moment the magazine seats, and skip the rest.
- Counters: shoot the sprinter during the approach (they cannot shoot back), melee as they arrive; a sprinter who reaches Melee band at full shield against a full-shield enemy loses the melee trade if the defender shoots first.

### Armor Lock

- Basics: invulnerable for up to about 5 s while held; cannot move; releases an EMP burst that pops nearby shields and stalls vehicles; the meter drains while held and recharges slowly.
- Advanced: lock when stuck (the grenade falls off), when a rocket or laser is incoming, when a vehicle is about to splatter (the vehicle is EMP'd and often flipped), when a sword lunges (the sword user is stunned and takes damage).
- Sneaky: armor lock negates fall damage, so the bot can drop from any height by locking just before landing; it negates a Wraith mortar and a Scorpion shell completely; releasing early the instant an enemy melees you catches them in the EMP for a free melee back; armor lock blocks all damage including assassinations, which is why the counter is distance, not contact. Armor lock while standing on a flag stand or hill still counts as being there (the hill keeps scoring, the flag cannot be taken while locked). Locking inside a doorway blocks the doorway. The EMP on release strips the shields of everyone within about 10 ft, so locking next to two enemies and releasing is a double shield pop.
- Counters: never melee or shoot a locked player; back off to Medium band, reload, throw a plasma grenade to land beside them (the splash hits when the lock ends), aim at the head; the lock ends when the meter drains or they release, and they are weak for the next second. Watch the meter glow: a long lock means an empty meter and no second lock.

### Jetpack

- Basics: about 2 s of thrust per charge, recharges on the ground; the bot can shoot while flying.
- Advanced: reach high holds (Sword Base top, Countdown's lift ceiling, Reflection's top walkway, Pinnacle's tower); fly over walls with the flag; hover above a doorway and drop on whoever walks through.
- Sneaky: feather the thrust (short taps) to float longer and lower than a full burn; a jetpack negates fall damage if tapped before landing; jetpacking straight up over a corner lets the bot shoot down on a camper without entering their lane; a jetpack plus a hammer is a vertical hammer drop that catches jumpers and crouchers alike; flying over the map's kill barriers is blocked, but flying over a kill ball is not; the jetpack can carry the bot across Boneyard's and Spire's gaps that no vehicle crosses. A jetpacker over an armor-locked enemy drops a grenade straight down on them.
- Counters: a jetpacker cannot strafe; shoot them in the air with the DMR or needle rifle; the needler supercombines on them; shoot the jetpacker's landing spot. A flying flag carrier is a slow, straight-line target.

### Active Camo

- Basics: near-invisibility while still or crouch-walking; moving fast or shooting fades it; it jams the motion trackers of everyone nearby, including the user.
- Advanced: crouch-walk to a corner on the enemy's route with a sword or shotgun; sit at the flag stand invisible; camo plus a sniper from a dark corner.
- Sneaky: the radar jam is the tell: when every dot on the radar turns to static, a camo user is within range, so the counter-bot stops and looks for the shimmer. A camo user who stands perfectly still is invisible even to a careful look, but a camo user who crouch-walks shows a faint outline at Short band; aim at the outline's head. A camo bot that throws a grenade reveals itself at the throw, so throw and move. Camo plus hologram sends a visible decoy while the real player is invisible: enemies shoot the hologram. Camo does not hide the bot from Promethean-style vision (not in Reach) but does hide from the radar completely when still.
- Counters: melee the suspected spot; grenade it; watch for the shimmer against the sky; in objective games camp the objective the camo user wants, not the lane.

### Hologram

- Basics: a decoy that runs to the aimed point for about 10 s, shows on enemy radar as a player, and is destroyed by damage.
- Advanced: send it around a corner to see if the corner is held (if it gets shot, it is); send it at a sniper to draw the shot and then peek; send it running next to the flag carrier so chasers split fire.
- Sneaky: a hologram triggers nothing physical but it does cause AI and many players to swing: an armor-lock user locks at it, a sword user lunges at it, a sniper fires at it. Send the hologram across a man cannon or lift and enemies pre-aim the landing while the bot takes the ramp. Send it to stand in a hill or on a flag: a hologram in a hill does not score, but enemies push it. A hologram's path shows on radar as a moving dot, so it sells a fake flank while the real bot goes the other way. The hologram walks straight through grenades and keeps going, which marks it as fake to a careful watcher (holograms do not react).
- Counters: a target that runs straight without shooting or strafing is a hologram; shoot it once (it dies to any damage) and ignore the rest; expect the real player from the other side.

### Drop Shield

- Basics: a dome like the Halo 3 Bubble Shield that also heals health (not only shields) for anyone inside; breaks under sustained fire or after about 15 s.
- Advanced: drop on the flag stand or hill; drop on a teammate who is low; drop in a doorway to block a lane.
- Sneaky: it heals the bleed-through health that Reach otherwise never restores below a third, so a drop shield is a mobile health pack for the whole team; it blocks a vehicle splatter and a Ghost boost (the Ghost stops); it negates fall damage for anyone who lands in it; a drop shield plus armor lock lets one bot tank a doorway almost indefinitely; enemies inside it also heal, so the dome is bait for a melee trade the bot starts with a full shield.
- Counters: shoot it down (it has health), throw a grenade inside, walk in and melee; a Power Drain-style EMP does not exist in Reach but the grenade launcher's EMP round pops it.

### Evade

- Basics: a quick two-roll dodge (Elites; Spartans in some variants); covers about 15 ft per roll.
- Advanced: dodge grenades and rockets; close distance on a shotgun user; break a strafe to dodge a sniper.
- Sneaky: evade cancels the reload animation after the magazine seats (like sprint) and cancels the end of a melee swing; evading off a ledge adds distance to a jump; the roll ends in a fixed spot, so an evader who rolls toward cover is predictable, but one who rolls sideways mid-fight resets the enemy's aim twice; evade into a sword lunge sidesteps it entirely.
- Counters: aim where the roll ends; a roll has no invulnerability, only movement.

### Loadout synergies the bot should pick

- Sprint plus shotgun or sword for rushing; armor lock plus grenade launcher for vehicle maps; jetpack plus DMR or needle rifle for vertical maps; camo plus sniper or shotgun for ambushes; hologram plus sniper for baiting; drop shield plus DMR for anchoring an objective; evade plus needler or plasma rifle for Elites in Invasion.

## Halo 4 armor abilities, packages and upgrades in depth

Halo 4 loadouts give every spawn an ability plus a Tactical Package and a Support Upgrade. Sprint is universal, and the abilities are shorter and weaker than Reach's. The hidden rules are mostly about what the perks change.

### Abilities

- Promethean Vision: a pulse that shows enemy outlines through walls for a few seconds. Sneaky: it also marks holograms as holograms (they render differently from real players), it reveals a camo user plainly, and the pulse is visible to enemies as a glow on the user, so pulse behind cover. Use it before every corner and every room push, and to see which way a flag carrier went. Counter: assume you are seen when the pulse sound plays and move; the user cannot shoot accurately while it is active.
- Thruster Pack: a short burst in any direction. Sneaky: thruster at the top of a jump adds height and distance (crouch-jump then thrust clears ledges on Haven, Adrift and Solace that no plain jump reaches); thruster sideways breaks a BR four-shot rhythm and the enemy's lead; thruster backward out of a sword or hammer lunge dodges it; thruster cancels the end of a reload once the magazine seats; thruster with the flag is allowed and is the fastest flag dodge in the game; thruster into a vehicle's path is a splatter dodge. Counter: aim where the thrust ends (it is short and straight).
- Hardlight Shield: a forward shield that blocks projectiles and lunges. Sneaky: it blocks a sword lunge and the sword user bounces off, it stops light vehicles (the Ghost halts on it), it absorbs a rocket, and the flag carrier cannot raise it while holding the flag, so the escort carries it instead. Walk it into a doorway as a mobile wall for teammates behind; drop it the instant the enemy reloads and shoot. Counter: grenades at the feet, flank behind it (it covers only the front), or wait for the shield to drain.
- Hologram: as Reach, with one addition: Promethean Vision exposes it, so hologram baits only enemies without PV. Send it at a sniper or into a hill.
- Active Camo: as Reach, but shorter and it still jams nearby radar. Camo plus a shotgun or scattershot at an objective; camo plus a sniper from a dark corner. Counter: PV, the radar static tell, and melee the shimmer.
- Jetpack: shorter than Reach's; the bot can shoot while flying. Use for Adrift and Haven top plays and for crossing Ragnarok's river with the flag; a jetpack plus a grenade over a hold drops the grenade straight down. Counter: no strafe in the air; DMR or BR the flyer.
- Autosentry: a hovering turret that fires at enemies in its view. Sneaky: it shows enemies by shooting them, so it is a flank alarm when placed behind the bot's hold; placed in a doorway it blocks the view and absorbs the first shots of anyone pushing; placed on a flag stand it fires at the puller. Enemies target it first, which is the point: a sentry is a decoy. Counter: shoot it (it dies fast), or approach from outside its arc.
- Regeneration Field: heals shields of anyone inside and gives a short damage-resist glow. Sneaky: stacks with the Shielding upgrade for faster recharge, heals the whole team on a Dominion base, and is bait for an enemy to walk in (fight them inside it). Counter: grenades in, or step inside too.

### Tactical Packages (one per loadout)

- Mobility: unlimited sprint. Best for flag runners and BTB rotations; the bot never stops to recover.
- Shielding: faster shield recharge. Best for the anchor and the sniper who trades at Medium band.
- Resupply: pick up grenades from dead players. Best for grenade-heavy play on small maps; without it, dead bodies drop no grenades (a key Halo 4 difference from Halo 3).
- AA Efficiency: faster ability recharge. Best for thruster and PV users.
- Grenadier: carry an extra grenade.
- Firepower: a second primary weapon in the secondary slot (two rifles).
- Fast Track: faster XP; no in-game effect.
- Requisition: re-roll ordnance drops.
- Wheelman: vehicles take less EMP stall and recharge faster.

### Support Upgrades (one per loadout)

- Awareness: radar stays on while scoped. Best for snipers: the bot can scope and still see flankers.
- Dexterity: faster reload and weapon switch. Best for shotgun and sniper users; reload-cancelling matters less.
- Explosives: bigger grenade radius, less self-damage. Best for small maps.
- Ammo: more spare ammo for the loadout weapons.
- Sensor: longer radar range. Best for flank watchers.
- Ordnance Priority: more frequent personal ordnance.
- Gunner: turrets do not overheat as fast and the carrier moves faster with a detached turret.
- Stability: less flinch when shot while scoped. Best for DMR duels at Long band.
- Drop Recon: shows ordnance drops earlier and marks them.
- Nemesis: marks the last player who killed you.
- Stealth: faster assassinations and quieter movement, and the bot shows less on PV. Best for camo and flank bots.

### Ordnance tricks

- Personal ordnance offers three choices on a timer; the bot picks by map and situation: a power weapon when the fight is at Medium band or longer, Overshield when the team is pushing, Speed Boost for a flag run, Damage Boost for a close-range duel.
- Random map ordnance drops are announced and marked; the pod lands a few seconds later; the bot that reaches the pod second throws a grenade at the pod, not at the first bot.
- An ordnance pod landing on a player kills them; the drop marker is also a drop zone to avoid.
- Requisition re-rolls once; use it when none of the three options fits the map.

### Loadout synergies the bot should pick

- Rusher: Thruster, Mobility, Dexterity, with a BR plus shotgun or sword.
- Anchor: Regeneration Field or Hardlight Shield, Shielding, Stability, DMR plus magnum.
- Flanker: Active Camo, Mobility, Stealth, BR plus boltshot.
- Sniper: PV or Hardlight, Shielding, Awareness, DMR plus sniper when picked up.
- Flag runner: Thruster or Jetpack, Mobility, Dexterity.
- Vehicle crew: Hardlight, Wheelman, Gunner.

### Overshield tricks

- Charge-up invulnerability (H2, H3): while the overshield is charging (about 2 s after pickup) the player takes no damage. The bot grabs OS under fire deliberately: it steps onto it as the enemy commits and the enemy's burst is wasted. A sticky on an OS grabber during charge-up does nothing.
- CE's overshield decays whether shot or not, so the bot uses it immediately (push now); it also makes the CE pistol a four-shot instead of three, so the enemy's timing is off.
- An OS player should push into the enemy's held position and trade: two enemies spend a full magazine each on the OS and the second teammate cleans up.
- Denial: the powerup cannot be destroyed, but the bot can stand on the spawn and grab it the instant it appears, throw a Trip Mine on the spawn (H3), or place a hologram on it (Reach) so the enemy pre-aims the wrong body.
- A Power Drain (H3) or armor lock EMP (Reach) strips an overshield instantly; a plasma pistol charged shot does too.

### Active Camo tricks

- CE: camo does not fade when shooting or moving, and the camo player still shows on the motion tracker, so the counter is radar and listening; camo plus the CE pistol or rockets is a free kill or two. Grab camo and go to the enemy's sniper.
- H2 and H3: camo fades with speed and fire; crouch-walking keeps it, a sniper scope shows the shimmer clearly, and the camo user still shows on enemy radar when moving fast. The sneaky play is to stand still at the enemy's power weapon spawn and take it when they walk up.
- H3: camo plus a sword or shotgun at a doorway; camo plus a flag pull (the carrier keeps the camo but the flag's waypoint shows everyone where it is, so camo helps only until the pull).
- Reach and H4: the loadout version, covered above, plus the map version on some maps which does not jam radar.
- Camo and vehicles: a camo driver is visible because the vehicle is visible; a camo passenger is a hidden gunner.

### Custom Powerup tricks

- In Grifball-style and party variants the custom powerup often grants a speed or damage boost; the bot reads the variant and treats it as the equivalent H4 ordnance boost.
- In some MLG Reach variants the custom powerup is removed; in some H3 customs it grants an overshield and camo together, which is the only time the bot should rush a sniper head-on.

### Damage Boost and Speed Boost (H4)

- Damage Boost turns the BR into a three-shot and the DMR into a four-shot, and makes melee a near one-hit; the bot pushes to Short band immediately.
- Speed Boost is the best flag-running powerup in the game and also speeds reloads; the bot that gets it in CTF goes for the flag at once.
- Both glow visibly, so the carrier is a target; use the 30 s, do not save it.

### Powerup priority for the bot

1. Overshield when the team is about to push or is in a slayer trade.
2. Camo when the bot is a flanker or sniper, or when the enemy has a sniper to kill.
3. Speed Boost for any objective run; Damage Boost for a close-range map.
4. Deny the enemy's powerup spawn with presence, a Trip Mine or a hologram before taking your own on a timer tie.

## Bot decision rules for equipment and abilities

The ability or equipment decision sits in the bot decision stack (main tab, section 10) below Survive and above Engage: the bot checks whether an ability changes the fight before it chooses a target, and it checks again at every state change.

### Use-now triggers

| Situation | H3 equipment | Reach ability | H4 ability |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Stuck by grenade | Bubble Shield (blocks the blast for teammates, not the stuck bot) | Armor Lock | Thruster toward the enemy (take them with you) |
| Rocket or laser incoming | Bubble Shield, Deployable Cover | Armor Lock | Hardlight Shield |
| Vehicle about to splatter | Bubble Shield (vehicle stops) | Armor Lock (vehicle EMP'd) | Hardlight Shield or Thruster sideways |
| Sword or hammer lunge incoming | Nothing stops it; strafe back | Armor Lock (attacker stunned) or Evade | Hardlight Shield or Thruster back |
| Shield popped, no cover | Bubble Shield, Regenerator | Drop Shield, Sprint away | Regeneration Field, Thruster to cover |
| About to push a held room | Power Drain, Flare, Radar Jammer | Hologram first, then Sprint | Promethean Vision, then Hologram |
| Enemy sniper scoped on a lane | Flare, Deployable Cover across the lane | Hologram at the sniper, Jetpack over | Hologram, Thruster across |
| Holding an objective | Regenerator, Trip Mine on the route, Bubble on plant | Drop Shield, Armor Lock on the stand | Regeneration Field, Autosentry on the flank |
| Carrying the flag or ball | Bubble Shield on the run, Grav Lift over a wall | Sprint, Jetpack over a wall | Thruster, Jetpack, Mobility |
| Stationary enemy in sight | Grav Lift under them, Trip Mine on their exit | Hologram to pull them | Autosentry to pin them |
| Enemy vehicle stopped | Power Drain, then board | Armor Lock beside it, then board | Thruster onto it, board |
| Enemy has Overshield | Power Drain | Plasma pistol, Armor Lock EMP | Plasma pistol |

### Hold triggers (do not use yet)

- The ability is the bot's only escape and the fight is not yet lost: keep Armor Lock, Thruster and Bubble Shield for the moment the shield pops, not before.
- The bot is alone and the equipment is a team item (Regenerator, Drop Shield, Deployable Cover): hold it for the regroup.
- Radar Jammer and Flare blind the team too: hold until the team is committed to the push.
- Hologram is cheap and recharges: use it freely, but never when the bot is already seen (it reveals nothing then).

### Bait rules

- Equipment and powerup spawns are bait by default: a bot approaching a spawn checks for a Trip Mine, a camper and a hologram before stepping on it.
- A Regenerator or Drop Shield in the open is a trap: the bot shoots whoever is inside only from outside Short band, or throws a grenade in.
- A dropped flag with a Trip Mine beside it is not picked up until the mine is shot.
- A hologram running straight is shot once and ignored.
- An armor-locked enemy is a timer, not a target: back off, reload, nade beside, aim head.

A bot carrying a higher-priority item does not swap for a lower one; a bot with an ability that matches its role keeps it across respawns unless the mode phase changes (defense to attack, or the enemy fields vehicles).

### Things most players never learn (quick list)

- H3 Overshield charge-up makes the grabber invulnerable for about 2 s; grab it under fire.
- H3 Trip Mines ride on vehicles and on the Elephant; a boarded vehicle with a mine on it is a bomb.
- H3 Grav Lift launches enemies, grenades and Mongooses; a frag in a lift lobs onto a roof.
- H3 Bubble Shield stops vehicles dead, blocks lasers, lets grenades out but not in, and lets swords in.
- H3 Power Drain inside a Bubble Shield or Regenerator cancels both.
- H3 Deployable Cover is a step: stand on it to reach ledges.
- Reach Armor Lock negates fall damage and sword lunges and flips the vehicle that hits you; release early next to a meleeing enemy for a free EMP.
- Reach Jetpack feathered floats longer; it also negates fall damage.
- Reach camo's radar static is the tell; stand still and look for the shimmer.
- Reach and H4 holograms give away a corner: if it gets shot, the corner is held.
- Reach Drop Shield heals bleed-through health that nothing else restores.
- Reach Sprint, Evade, and H4 Thruster all cancel the tail of a reload once the magazine seats.
- H4 Promethean Vision exposes holograms and camo, but the pulse glow exposes the user.
- H4 Hardlight Shield stops light vehicles and sword lunges; grenades at the feet beat it.
- H4 Autosentry is a flank alarm and a decoy more than a gun.
- H4 bodies drop no grenades without the Resupply package; grenade economy is a loadout choice.
- H4 ordnance pods kill players they land on.
